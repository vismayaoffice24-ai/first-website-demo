Kidney Disease Management

A steady, structured medical strategy designed to slow functional decline, manage complications, and protect remaining kidney tissue over time.

What it includes

Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) requires continuous, multidisciplinary supervision rather than sporadic emergency visits. Dr. Neelima's management program includes regular monitoring of renal parameters (eGFR, serum creatinine, urea, electrolytes), rigorous blood pressure control utilizing renoprotective ACE inhibitors or ARBs, optimal glycemic control for diabetic patients, screening and treatment of secondary complications (such as renal anemia using erythropoietin-stimulating agents and iron therapy, metabolic acidosis with bicarbonate, and chronic kidney disease-mineral bone disorder using phosphate binders and active vitamin D), comprehensive medication audits to prevent drug toxicity, and individualized nutritional counseling.

Who it can help

This program is tailored for patients living with Chronic Kidney Disease across Stages 1, 2, 3, and 4, individuals with diabetic kidney disease or hypertensive nephrosclerosis, patients with chronic glomerulonephritis or polycystic kidney disease, and those with persistent proteinuria or unexplained progressive creatinine elevation.

What to expect

During regular appointments at MIMS Hospital, Kannur, Dr. Neelima will track your lab trends over time to identify any rate of decline early. Every consultation provides a clear, understandable discussion of your test numbers, reviews your medication tolerances, and establishes achievable health goals for blood pressure, blood glucose, dietary salt, and fluid intake. With scheduled reviews, changes are detected promptly, enabling proactive adjustments that keep you healthy and out of the hospital.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should a CKD patient see a nephrologist?

In early stages (CKD Stages 1–2), check-ups every 6 to 12 months are often sufficient. In Stage 3, consultations are typically scheduled every 3 to 6 months. In advanced stages (Stage 4), visits every 1 to 2 months are vital to closely monitor electrolytes, anemia, and prepare for renal replacement therapies.

Can medications really slow down kidney decline?

Yes. Clinical trials confirm that modern therapies—such as SGLT2 inhibitors, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and non-steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists—substantially reduce intraglomerular pressure, decrease proteinuria, and significantly prolong the years before kidney failure develops.

Why do kidney patients develop anemia?

Healthy kidneys produce the hormone erythropoietin (EPO), which stimulates red blood cell production in bone marrow. As renal tissue scars, EPO production drops, causing anemia. Dr. Neelima monitors hemoglobin and prescribes targeted therapy to restore energy and vitality.
