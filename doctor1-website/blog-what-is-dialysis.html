What Is Dialysis? A Practical Patient Guide

By Dr. Neelima · Nephrologist / Kidney Specialist, MIMS Hospital, Kannur

For patients and families facing advanced kidney failure, hearing the word "dialysis" can trigger anxiety. However, dialysis is not an end point—it is a life-sustaining, restorative medical therapy that performs the essential tasks healthy kidneys normally handle: filtering toxins, balancing electrolytes, and removing excess fluid from the body.

When Is Dialysis Needed?

Dialysis is generally recommended when kidneys reach Stage 5 Chronic Kidney Disease (End-Stage Renal Disease), typically when the estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR) drops below 10 to 15 mL/min, or when acute kidney injury (AKI) causes severe fluid buildup, dangerously high potassium, or metabolic acidosis that cannot be managed with medications alone. Dialysis allows patients to relieve debilitating uremic symptoms, sleep comfortably, and maintain their daily activities.

The Two Primary Types of Dialysis

There are two main modalities of dialysis, each with specific clinical considerations:

  • Hemodialysis: In hemodialysis, blood is gently drawn from a vascular access (such as an arteriovenous fistula or temporary catheter), routed through an external artificial kidney machine (dialyzer) that washes out toxins and excess fluid across a specialized semipermeable membrane, and returned safely to the body. Most patients undergo hemodialysis in a hospital or dialysis center three times weekly, with each session lasting roughly four hours.
  • Peritoneal Dialysis (PD): In peritoneal dialysis, the natural lining of your abdomen (the peritoneum) acts as the biological filter. A sterile cleansing fluid (dialysate) is introduced into the abdominal cavity through a soft, flexible catheter. After dwelling for a prescribed period to absorb toxins, the fluid is drained out and replaced. PD can often be performed at home or overnight during sleep.

Living Well on Dialysis

Starting dialysis requires adjustments in daily fluid intake, dietary salt, potassium, and phosphorus consumption. However, with compassionate nephrology supervision, individualized dialysis prescriptions, and modern facilities like those at MIMS Hospital, Kannur, patients on dialysis continue to spend quality time with loved ones, travel, and enjoy meaningful lives while awaiting kidney transplantation.
