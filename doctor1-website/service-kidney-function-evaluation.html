Kidney Function Evaluation

A complete diagnostic assessment of your kidneys' filtering efficiency, structure, and metabolic health, explained in plain language.

What it includes

A comprehensive kidney function evaluation begins with an unhurried clinical review of your symptoms, medical background, past hospitalizations, and current prescription medications. Diagnostic testing includes blood assessments (serum creatinine, blood urea nitrogen, serum electrolytes including potassium and sodium, calcium, and phosphorus), calculation of your estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR), and detailed urinalysis (including spot urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio and microscopic sediment analysis to detect occult blood or cellular casts). When necessary, high-resolution renal ultrasound imaging is arranged to inspect kidney size, parenchymal thickness, and rule out anatomical blockages or stones.

Who it can help

This service is vital for individuals who have received abnormal or borderline blood or urine test results, people noticing persistent swelling in the legs, ankles, or under the eyes, those experiencing foamy or dark urine, individuals diagnosed with diabetes or high blood pressure, patients with a family history of kidney disease or stones, and anyone on long-term medications (such as NSAID painkillers or certain cardiac drugs) that require regular renal safety monitoring.

What to expect

During your consultation at MIMS Hospital, Kannur, Dr. Neelima will examine your previous medical records and explain what each test value means in clear, non-technical terms. If further diagnostic tests or imaging are warranted, they will be organized efficiently through MIMS Hospital's advanced laboratory facilities. You will leave with a clear, written action plan detailing dietary guidelines, blood pressure targets, medication reviews, and scheduled follow-up timelines.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to fast before a kidney function blood test?

Fast is generally not required for basic serum creatinine and electrolyte tests, though drinking a normal amount of water is recommended so you are not dehydrated. However, if your doctor is checking fasting blood glucose or a lipid profile at the same visit, an 8 to 10 hour fast may be needed.

What is the difference between serum creatinine and eGFR?

Serum creatinine is the raw concentration of a muscle waste product measured in your blood. eGFR (estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate) is a calculated score factoring in your creatinine, age, and sex to estimate the percentage of total filtering capacity your kidneys possess.

Why is a urine test just as important as a blood test for kidneys?

Blood tests tell us how well kidneys clear waste, but urine tests reveal whether delicate filters are physically leaking protein (albumin) or blood cells. In conditions like diabetic nephropathy, urine protein leaks often appear years before blood creatinine starts to rise.
