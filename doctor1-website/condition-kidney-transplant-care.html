Kidney Transplant Care

Dedicated medical care, donor-recipient compatibility evaluation, and vigilant long-term follow-up to protect your transplanted kidney.

What it is

Kidney transplantation is the preferred, definitive treatment modality for patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD), offering significantly enhanced longevity, freedom from regular dialysis sessions, and a vastly improved quality of life. However, successfully receiving a donor kidney is only the beginning of a lifelong journey. A transplanted kidney (allograft) requires meticulous, structured clinical supervision to balance the immune system, preserve filtration, monitor for rejection, and prevent opportunistic infections.

Signs to report immediately

Post-transplant recipients must remain alert to subtle changes in health. Contact Dr. Neelima or your transplant team immediately if you notice:
• Sudden reduction in daily urine output or dark-colored urine.
• Rapid, unexplained weight gain or swelling in your ankles, legs, or face.
• Fever of 100°F (37.8°C) or higher, chills, persistent sore throat, or cough.
• Tenderness, pain, swelling, or redness over the graft site in the lower abdomen.
• New or worsening high blood pressure readings.
• Severe fatigue, flu-like aches, or persistent nausea and vomiting that prevents you from taking your immunosuppressive medications.

How a nephrologist can help

Dr. Neelima is an active member of the Indian Society of Organ Transplantation (ISOT) with extensive clinical experience in kidney transplant medicine. At MIMS Hospital, Kannur, she provides comprehensive pre- and post-transplant care:
Pre-Transplant: Thorough clinical evaluation of potential transplant recipients and prospective living donors, counseling on organ donation pathways, and optimization of cardiovascular and metabolic fitness.
Post-Transplant: Precise drug therapeutic monitoring (measuring blood trough levels of tacrolimus, cyclosporine, or mycophenolate), screening for subtle acute cellular or antibody-mediated rejection, managing post-transplant diabetes and hypertension, preventing cytomegalovirus (CMV) and BK virus infections, and providing individualized guidance on nutrition, food hygiene, and safe daily living.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is it vital to take immunosuppressive medications exactly on time?

Immunosuppressant drugs keep your body's immune system from recognizing the donor kidney as foreign tissue. Missing doses or altering timings can cause drug levels to drop, triggering acute rejection episodes that can permanently damage the allograft.

How often do I need blood tests after a kidney transplant?

During the first few months post-transplant, blood tests (serum creatinine, electrolytes, immunosuppressant trough levels) are checked weekly or bi-weekly. As the graft stabilizes, visits gradually space out to monthly, and eventually every 2 to 3 months for lifelong monitoring.

What lifestyle precautions are essential for transplant recipients?

Because immunosuppressive therapy lowers infection defense, recipients must drink only boiled or filtered water, eat freshly cooked food, avoid raw salads or unpasteurized dairy, wash hands frequently, avoid close contact with sick individuals, and wear a mask in crowded indoor spaces.
