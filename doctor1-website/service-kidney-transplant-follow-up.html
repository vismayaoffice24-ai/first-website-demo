Kidney Transplant Follow-up Care

Continuous, dedicated post-transplant medical supervision to ensure optimal immunosuppressant balance and long-term allograft survival.

What it includes

Post-transplant care is an ongoing medical partnership to ensure that your transplanted kidney functions reliably for years to come. Dr. Neelima's specialized follow-up service includes regular blood testing (serum creatinine, eGFR, electrolytes, complete blood count), therapeutic drug monitoring to maintain precise blood trough levels of immunosuppressant medications (such as tacrolimus, cyclosporine, and mycophenolate mofetil), vigilant screening for early cellular or antibody-mediated rejection, monitoring for viral reactivation (such as Cytomegalovirus [CMV] and BK polyomavirus), management of post-transplant metabolic complications (including new-onset diabetes after transplantation [NODAT], hypertension, and dyslipidemia), and comprehensive vaccination and infection prevention counseling.

Who it can help

This service is designed specifically for individuals who have received a living or deceased donor kidney transplant—whether recently discharged from surgery or living with a mature graft for many years—who seek dedicated, accessible nephrological care and monitoring in Kannur and northern Kerala.

What to expect

At every follow-up visit at MIMS Hospital, Kannur, Dr. Neelima performs a thorough clinical examination, checks your blood pressure, inspects the graft site in your lower abdomen, and reviews your recent laboratory test trends. You will receive an updated, easy-to-follow medication schedule detailing exact doses and timings, dietary and food safety reminders, and clear instructions on specific symptoms (such as fever, swelling, or urine reduction) that warrant urgent contact. With scheduled visits, you gain confidence that your transplanted kidney is receiving expert, vigilant protection.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a drug trough level and why must blood be drawn before taking morning medicines?

A trough level measures the lowest concentration of an immunosuppressive medication in your bloodstream, right before your next dose. Taking your morning pill before the blood draw artificially spikes the result, leading to incorrect dosage decisions. Always wait until after your blood is drawn to take your morning dose on test days.

What are the most common signs of kidney transplant rejection?

Rejection can sometimes occur silently without pain, detectable only by rising serum creatinine. When symptoms do occur, they may include decreased urine output, sudden weight gain or swelling, tenderness or fullness over the transplanted kidney, fever, and rising blood pressure.

Can I take over-the-counter pain relievers after a kidney transplant?

Transplant recipients must strictly avoid non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) such as ibuprofen, diclofenac, and naproxen, as they reduce blood flow to the transplanted kidney and interact dangerously with calcineurin inhibitors. Paracetamol is generally safe in recommended doses, but always check with Dr. Neelima first.
