Frequently Asked Questions

When should I see a kidney specialist (nephrologist)?

You should consider consulting a nephrologist if routine blood tests reveal elevated serum creatinine, decreased estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR), or if urine tests detect protein (albumin) or blood (hematuria). Other key indications include persistent swelling in the feet, ankles, or around the eyes, unexplained changes in urine frequency or appearance (such as foamy urine), difficult-to-control high blood pressure, recurring kidney stones, or longstanding diabetes.

What should I bring to my first consultation?

Please bring all recent blood and urine test reports, renal ultrasound or CT imaging films/reports, hospital discharge summaries if recently admitted, a complete and up-to-date list of all prescription and over-the-counter medications you are taking, and a written list of any symptoms or questions you wish to discuss.

Does an elevated creatinine level always mean permanent kidney disease?

Not necessarily. While serum creatinine is an important marker of kidney function, acute spikes can occur due to temporary factors such as severe dehydration, urinary tract blockages, acute infections, strenuous muscle breakdown, or certain medications (such as non-steroidal anti-inflammatory painkillers). Dr. Neelima conducts a comprehensive clinical evaluation and repeat testing to differentiate between reversible acute kidney injury (AKI) and chronic kidney disease (CKD).

How can I protect my kidneys on a daily basis?

Protecting your kidneys involves maintaining well-controlled blood pressure (typically below 130/80 mmHg) and blood sugar levels, drinking adequate clean water daily, moderating dietary sodium, eating a balanced diet rich in whole foods, avoiding habitual or unprescribed use of pain medications (NSAIDs), refraining from smoking, and undergoing annual preventive blood and urine kidney screenings if you have diabetes or hypertension.

What is the difference between a Nephrologist and a Urologist?

A nephrologist is an internal medicine specialist who manages the medical health, filtering capacity, and systemic diseases of the kidneys—such as chronic kidney disease, diabetic kidney disease, electrolyte imbalances, blood pressure issues, and dialysis care. A urologist is a surgeon who treats structural and surgical disorders of the male and female urinary tract, including surgical stone removal, prostate conditions, and urinary bladder surgery. Nephrologists and urologists frequently collaborate closely.
