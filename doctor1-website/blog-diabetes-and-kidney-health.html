Diabetes & Kidneys

Understanding Diabetes & Your Kidney Health

By Dr. Neelima · Nephrologist / Kidney Specialist, MIMS Hospital, Kannur

Diabetes is the leading cause of chronic kidney disease (CKD) and end-stage renal disease worldwide. In India, where diabetes prevalence continues to climb, diabetic kidney disease (historically termed diabetic nephropathy) affects nearly one in three people living with diabetes. Understanding how high blood sugar impacts your kidneys—and why early screening is critical—empowers you to preserve renal function for decades to come.

How Diabetes Affects the Kidneys

Inside each kidney are hundreds of thousands of microscopic blood-filtering clusters known as glomeruli. When blood glucose levels remain chronically elevated, these delicate capillary walls undergo structural stress. Over several years, high sugar induces oxidative damage, causes the filtering membranes to thicken, and creates increased pressure within the glomerular network (hyperfiltration). Eventually, the filters begin to leak valuable blood proteins—predominantly albumin—into the urine, while waste products remain trapped in the bloodstream.

Why Early Detection is Critical

In its initial stages, diabetic kidney disease produces no physical symptoms. A patient may feel energetic and healthy even while their filtering units are under severe strain. By the time physical symptoms such as ankle swelling, morning facial puffiness, foamy urine, or declining appetite appear, significant nephron scarring may have already taken place. This is why every person diagnosed with Type 2 diabetes should undergo annual kidney screening starting from the time of diabetes diagnosis, while those with Type 1 diabetes should begin screening within five years of diagnosis.

The Essential Screening Tests

Effective diabetic kidney monitoring requires two straightforward lab tests:

  • Urine Albumin-to-Creatinine Ratio (uACR): This test detects tiny amounts of protein in the urine (microalbuminuria) long before standard urine dipsticks can register it. Microalbuminuria is the earliest warning flag of diabetic kidney involvement.
  • Serum Creatinine & eGFR: A simple blood draw that estimates how efficiently your kidneys are clearing metabolic waste products.

Protecting Your Kidneys

Fortunately, diabetic kidney damage can be prevented or substantially slowed. Maintaining your HbA1c below target (typically around 7.0%), keeping blood pressure controlled below 130/80 mmHg, adopting modern renoprotective medications (such as SGLT2 inhibitors and ACE inhibitors or ARBs), and eating a kidney-friendly diet with controlled salt and protein all provide proven defense for your renal filters.
