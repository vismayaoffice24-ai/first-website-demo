Dialysis Care & Clinical Management

Clinical guidance, personalized prescription, and attentive supervision for patients requiring hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis.

What it includes

Dr. Neelima's dialysis care service encompasses the full clinical spectrum of renal replacement therapy. This includes thorough clinical evaluation to determine if and when dialysis is needed, counseling patients and families on the advantages of hemodialysis versus peritoneal dialysis (PD), coordination of vascular access planning (such as creation and monitoring of arteriovenous fistulas/grafts or temporary central venous dialysis catheters), crafting individualized dialysis prescriptions (dialyzer membrane selection, blood flow rates, ultrafiltration targets, and session durations), monthly laboratory audits (Kt/V dialysis adequacy, hemoglobin, calcium, phosphorus, PTH, and albumin), and managing common dialysis-related concerns such as cramps, intradialytic hypotension, and anemia.

Who it can help

This service supports patients diagnosed with Stage 5 Chronic Kidney Disease (End-Stage Renal Disease), individuals experiencing acute kidney injury (AKI) who require temporary life-sustaining dialysis support while renal tissue recovers, patients currently on maintenance hemodialysis seeking a second opinion or comprehensive doctor review in Kannur, and individuals planning for peritoneal dialysis at home.

What to expect

Patients consulting Dr. Neelima at MIMS Hospital, Kannur, receive a warm, compassionate, and transparent assessment. Your doctor will carefully review your current blood tests, symptoms, fluid status, and vascular access site. If you are preparing to initiate treatment, Dr. Neelima will explain what the procedure entails step-by-step, outline fluid and dietary goals, answer all family questions, and coordinate your sessions with the dedicated, state-of-the-art dialysis unit at MIMS Hospital.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often is hemodialysis typically performed?

For most patients with chronic kidney failure, standard maintenance hemodialysis is performed three times per week, with each treatment session lasting approximately 3.5 to 4 hours, depending on individual body size, residual urine output, and clearance needs.

What is an arteriovenous (AV) fistula and why is it preferred?

An AV fistula is a surgically created connection between an artery and a vein, usually in your non-dominant forearm. It is considered the gold standard for hemodialysis because it lasts longer, provides reliable blood flow, and has a significantly lower risk of infection and clotting compared to artificial grafts or central venous catheters.

Can a person on dialysis live an active life?

Yes. With regular, adequate dialysis clearance, appropriate fluid and salt management, and proper anemia and bone mineral control, many patients continue working, spending time with family, traveling, and engaging in hobbies.
