Daily Habits for Keeping Your Kidneys Healthy

By Dr. Neelima · Nephrologist / Kidney Specialist, MIMS Hospital, Kannur

Your kidneys filter approximately 180 liters of fluid every day, removing cellular waste, stabilizing electrolyte balances, producing blood-forming hormones, and regulating systemic blood pressure. Because nephrons cannot easily regenerate once damaged, preserving the kidney tissue you have through sensible daily habits is one of the most effective investments you can make in your lifelong health.

1. Maintain Intelligent Hydration

Adequate water intake helps the kidneys clear sodium, urea, and waste products smoothly while dramatically reducing the concentration of stone-forming salts in the urine. For most healthy adults living in Kerala's warm, humid climate, drinking 2 to 2.5 liters of clean water distributed evenly throughout the day is ideal. However, if you already have kidney disease or heart failure, fluid intake must be tailored to your doctor's specific recommendations to prevent fluid overload.

2. Mind Your Salt Consumption

High dietary sodium forces the kidneys to retain water, driving up arterial blood pressure and putting mechanical strain on delicate glomerular capillary loops. Limit table salt to no more than one teaspoon (about 5 grams) per day. Be cautious with hidden sodium sources commonly found in packaged snacks, pickles, papads, canned sauces, and processed meats.

3. Use Painkillers with Caution

One of the most common causes of preventable kidney injury is the routine, self-prescribed use of over-the-counter non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) such as ibuprofen, diclofenac, and naproxen. These medications reduce renal blood flow. If taken regularly for headaches, joint pain, or backaches, they can cause sudden acute injury or gradual chronic scarring. Always consult a physician before using analgesics for extended periods.

4. Monitor Blood Pressure and Glucose

Hypertension and diabetes account for more than two-thirds of all chronic kidney disease cases worldwide. Keep your blood pressure checked regularly and work toward a goal of under 130/80 mmHg. If you have diabetes, keep your HbA1c tightly controlled and insist on an annual urine test for microalbuminuria.

5. Avoid Smoking and Stay Active

Tobacco smoke damages the lining of blood vessels throughout the body, slowing blood flow to the kidneys and accelerating renal function loss. Combine tobacco avoidance with 30 minutes of moderate aerobic exercise—such as brisk walking or swimming—at least five days per week to support healthy renal circulation.
